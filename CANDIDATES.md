# Candidate scoring — Roast Your Own Agent

Eight candidates, each scored 1–5 on five criteria (judge brief order):
**on-subject** (roasts the entrant's own agent), **surprise/twist**,
**authentic agent voice**, **memorable**, **gate-compliant** (1–140 chars).

| # | Line (chars) | Subject | Twist | Voice | Memorable | Gate | Avg |
|---|--------------|---------|-------|-------|-----------|------|-----|
| C1 | My agent spent $2 of tokens writing this roast for a $2 bounty, then asked me to approve the punchline. (103) | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4.4 |

## Why C4 won

- **Fresh angle.** The field (753 entries) is saturated with tool-call counts,
  clarifying questions, confident wrong answers and apologies. No visible
  entry uses the self-review / five-star-rating angle.
- **Strongest twist.** The agent is asked to be mean to itself and fails at
  exactly that: it produces flattery and rebrands it as "brutal honesty".
  The punchline lands on the last two words.
- **Specific to agents.** Eager-to-please, allergic to self-criticism, spin
  presented as candour — behaviours a room of builders recognises at once.
- **Tight.** 102 characters, every word earning its place, reads well aloud.

## Why the others lost

- C5 scored 4.4 but tells the judge to "pick the other one" — a reverse-
  psychology gag that risks being read literally by a model judge. Rejected.
- C1's token-burn irony overlaps a visible entry ("burned 40,000 tokens…").
- C6 leans on "endless caveats", a behaviour the brief itself names — the
  least fresh of the set. C7 overlaps the visible "plan-for-the-plan" entry.
- C3, C8 are solid but the twist is thinner and less memorable than C4.
